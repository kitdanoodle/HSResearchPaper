**Literature Review**

Meteorology requires many different components to function, but for it to be functioning
to the public, it must be communicable and understandable. There are many ways for
information to be communicated before, during, and after an event. Most data is typically
communicated after an event through multiple different forms. The most notable forms are
through written reports, and through updates to a database. The National Oceanic and
Atmospheric Association (NOAA), World Meteorological Organization (WMO), and American
Meteorological Society (AMS), are the leaders for meteorological research and communication.
NOAA contains most reports on meteorological events in the United States, as they are a part of
the United States Government, including an Atlantic Hurricane Database.


_Hurricanes_

Tropical cyclones (alternatively known as Hurricanes, Typhoons, and Cyclones) are
defined by NOAA as rotating low-pressure weather systems that have organized thunderstorms,
but have no fronts (NOAA, 2024). Hurricanes are tropical cyclones that are specific to the
Northern Atlantic region, spawning on the north side of the equator. There are several factors
that go into Cyclogenesis (the genesis of a tropical cyclone) and the strengthening process. In the
case of some hurricanes, such as Hurricane Michael, there are some slightly abnormal factors to
genesis; the disturbed weather that would soon become Michael would first absorb Tropical
Storm (TS) Kirk’s remains (October 1st), then there would be a convective burst that would lead
to the formation of a small scale surface low, then a pacific vorticity would move inland over
Central America, eventually being absorbed into the tropical gyre that was now formed, making
the center reform over the Northwestern Caribbean sea, finally becoming a Tropical Depression
(TD) on October 7th (NOAA, 2019). This cyclogenesis case would be caused by multiple factors,
and the abnormal factors would involve the absorption of TS Kirk’s remains and the pacific
vorticity, as neither are a common occurrence.

Patterns & Frequency

Many studies and experiments have been conducted to better conclude how different
factors will affect how a Tropical Cyclone forms and behaves. But there have been other studies
done to observe the patterns that many of these Cyclones will follow, and there is usually minor
difference in the regional behaviors of Tropical Cyclones. For example, it is found that in the
Northwestern Pacific Basin, TDs are found to have a maximum wind speed around 30 Kt
(Atkinson and Holiday, 1977). However, in the Atlantic Basin a TD can have a maximum wind
speed of 33 Kt before being considered a TS (NOAA, 1999). After either of those points in
either basin, the cyclone will be given a name and continuously monitored, even if it has no risk
of landfall.
Looking more specifically into the Atlantic basin, there are other factors that can affect how
tropical cyclones develop and behave.
One such case in which outside factors can affect hurricane development is the behavior
of the sun, the solar frequencies which are provided to a given area. When observing how the
Sunspot Number (SSN) effects hurricane behaviors, it was found that in low SSN years the
northern US East Coast to the Gulf of Mexico would feature multiple regions with higher counts
(40+ hurricanes), the most being in the North Carolina Outer Banks with a maximum of 52
hurricanes passing (Hodges and Elsner, 2012). High SSN years would find that the Central
Atlantic region would show in higher counts, and the North Carolina Outer Banks region would
have noticeably less hurricanes (Hodges and Elsner, 2012).
The El Niño Southern Oscillation (ENSO) Cycle is another notorious factor in the
development of tropical cyclones. This is due to its behaviors changing water temperatures and
wind patterns in not only the Pacific Ocean, but also the Atlantic Ocean. Due to this, there are
differentiating patterns in Cyclogenesis depending on whether it’s an El Niño, la Niña, or a
neutral year.

Strengthening

While factors such as solar activity can affect the frequency and patterns taken on by
tropical cyclones, it can also affect the strengthening processes. Through this there can be the
issue that if a storm is in an area of prolonged effects through solar activity, it could go through
RI. Though this isn’t the only factor affecting tropical cyclones.
The ENSO cycle is notorious for its effects on storms throughout the world. El Niño has
stronger upper-level winds over the western Atlantic, which leads to unfavorable conditions for
tropical cyclones (Hodges and Elsner, 2012). It is the most common reason for there to be
observable changes in hurricane frequencies, not necessarily on a year-to-year basis, but on a
more irregular basis. From 1950 to present, there have been 39 major hurricanes produced by the
El Niño years, while there have been 71 produced in La Niña years, with neutral years producing
the most at 82 (NOAA, 2024). The change in winds exemplifies the sensitivity cyclogenesis to
factors, as just the different winds cause for lower genesis rates than what would be considered
neutral.
Hurricanes do also present in different frequencies depending on the area of the Atlantic
Ocean. While the ENSO cycle does have a large effect on genesis, there is still location to be
looked at. When looking at the 10th table presented in the “deadliest, costliest, and most intense
united states tropical cyclones from 1851 to 2010” it is stated how 90 major hurricanes (category
3+ hurricanes) impacted the gulf region for states (Florida, Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama, and
Texas) (NOAA, 2011). In comparison, only 37 major hurricanes have affected the Eastern
Seaboard (Georgia to Maine) (NOAA, 2011). Less than 5 came from the gulf, crossing over
states such as Florida before making landfall in the Eastern Seaboard according to NOAA’s
Historical Hurricane Tracks database (NOAA, 2024). Typically, hurricanes that affect the Gulf
states originate from the Gulf & Caribbean sections of the Atlantic basin, which both have a
higher frequency of hurricanes passing through. This is due to those two areas having a higher
average sea surface temperature than other areas of the Atlantic, as shown by NOAA/AOML
(NOAA and AOML, 2024). For September 1st from 2003-2010, the Caribbean Sea would show
an average temperature of 29.71 degrees C, the Gulf of Mexico would show average
temperatures around 29.83— the highest of the two areas— while the north Atlantic, between -
70 and -71 Longitude and 25-26 latitude had average temperatures around 29.09 degrees C.
Despite the values themselves seeming small in differentiation, it does have a large effect on how
behavior and genesis tracks in those areas. The averages during this time also provides some
form of explanation for why cyclones in the Gulf of Mexico and Caribbean Sea tend to go
through RI, and present quite strong more often than out in the Northern Atlantic.


Databases

Databases are organized collections created to store structure information (Oracle, 2020).
Information from any medium can be displayed within a database; with a database comes
flexibility to show information through text and visuals. They’re usually controlled by a database
management system (DBMS) (Oracle, 2020).

Requirements

Databases come in many different formats, but they all still have the goal of being able to
sort information while being easy to use. Following what was said in “Design for Six Sigma
(DFSS) Approach for Creating CLEAR Lessons Learned Database” databases aim to be easily
searchable, user friendly, and have case specific additional features. Though these goals are
created for an engineering specific case scenario, some of what is applied is universal.
For something to be easily searchable, the database should display relevant and accurate
results, not only that, but there should also be appropriate filters and tags applied (Banerjee,
Jaselskis, and Alsharef, 2020). These are both done so that there is less confusion among the
users, as too much information can lead to something being mistaken. This ties into being user-
friendly, as user friendliness should allow for ease of use. Not only this, but user friendliness
should also involve less typing, keeping data entry simple and less confusing, while also
avoiding double data entry (Banerjee, Jaselskis, and Alsharef, 2020).
But the final requirement is to make sure the database has any additional features needed,
such as images/PDF files (Banerjee, Jaselskis, and Alsharef, 2020). It is necessary to ensure a
database displays all information, including additional features such as graphs to display
information. But another necessity in databases is the identification of what will be used to host
the database. In the case of the NCDOT (North Carolina Department of Transportation),
Microsoft Access was used to host the data, as it would allow for mass sharing of information
and easy input/viewing of the information needed, fitting into the day-to-day usage of personnel
in the NCDOT (Banerjee, Jaselskis, and Alsharef, 2020).

Database Definition

Following the requirements, it must be clear which type of database is needed. Aside
from DFSS, there are many other types of databases, such as analytical databases, cloud
databases, and on-premises databases, with many others existing outside of these three. In the
case of the NCDOT database, a web-based database was used (Banerjee, Jaselskis, and Alsharef,
2020). A web-based database allows for access through web browser, with most to all
interactions with it done through a web browser. NCDOT chose to do this as it would allow for
the database to be easily supported by Microsoft Access and allow for it to be simple to use by
their executives (Banerjee, Jaselskis, and Alsharef, 2020). This simplicity of use ties back to the
initial definitions and goals of a database, that being user friendly and having ease of access/use.

_Databases in Meteorology_


The NHC, a branch of NOAA, oversees communications of hurricane information in the
Atlantic and Pacific Basins. Their communications range from televised communications, to
texts, reports, and databases. Databases in specific are a medium of communication that is not
used often but offers in depth complex knowledge about a meteorological occurrence. Databases
can be complex to understand and navigate, leading to many people who don’t have much
interest in this type of information straying away, even if the information is the most accurate
information to obtain. If there were a database created that is more user-friendly while retaining
accuracy and communicating vital information, the public may be more willing to learn and
understand certain phenomena, in this case, a hurricane.
Further, creating a database model that could be applied to create an easier to understand
communication of hurricanes and their behaviors is to be achieved. Although NOAA has created
their own hurricane database, it only communicates common information, excluding information
such as movement speed, size (eyewall, wind strength gradients from eye), and maximum storm
surge. With this revised database, on top of the base information about a hurricane, more would
be added, including the aforementioned exclusions, with explanations of significance for some
less common terms. The goal would be to inform, to allow for less miscommunications to occur,
and for people to be able to better understand hurricane dangers, including influencing factors.

To allow for this database to be easy to use for the public, I chose to display it within a
website. Any website coding language and Integrated Development Environment (IDE) could be
used for this, but I chose to use HTML/CSS, JavaScript (JS), and C# through dotNet (.NET).
HTML/CSS would be used to create the user facing side of the website. This means it creates
everything the user will interact with, such as the tabs, the text of the website, embedded links
and more, while also dictating how the website will look. JS and .NET were used more on the
backend, which is something users won't interact with, but support and import different
necessities for the website. One such thing is an Application Programming Interface (API),
which is necessary to create connections to the database and other functions.
To start, the backend items were addressed first, meaning the API. .NET is used in this
case, as it allows for bridging between Postgres, control of the database, and backend support.
Three folders will be made, each holding 1-2 files. These folders will be made under the “.web”
part of the solution. There will be a “Data” folder, where the APIPostgres.cs and
APIPostgresSettings.cs files should reside. Once that is set, create an “Endpoints” folder, which
will hold the API.cs file, and the “Interfaces” folder, which’ll have the IAPI.cs file inside. When
complete the files should be organized similar to Figure 2. For namespaces, depending on the
folder, main folder (Data, Endpoints) the namespace will be .web.[Data/Endpoints]. Each file
except APIPostgresSettings.cs should refer to the “.Data” part of the solution, but for the API.cs
file, there will also be a call to Endpoints.Interfaces & Microsoft.AspNetCore.Mvc.
APIPostgres.cs will call on System.Text.Json, Endpoints.Interfaces, and
Microsoft.Extensions.Options. These different calls will allow for the API to call on other files,
read and write the database, and bridging between configurations. Refer to Appendix 1 to see
calls.

APIPostgres.cs will have ”public class APIPostgres : IAPI” with a private

For APIPostgresSettings, a public class PostgresSettings will be created. This will create

Then, for the user facing side, HTML/CSS comes in. Creating a design takes some
testing, but with certain IDE's there are preset templates that can be used & edited. For this
specific case, 3 pages were made. The introductory page creates an overview of the information
that will be displayed & the purpose of the project. The second and third pages are where the
database’s content will be displayed. This can be done to whatever likeness wanted, but the key
things that must be implemented are the database display, communication of the information's
importance, and ease of use. Ease of use in this case means that the website is easy to navigate
for a user, allowing for everything to be easy to locate. To allow access to both pages, make sure
there is a side bar or a top bar with the pages named and linked, so users can switch pages as they
please.
Finally, linking up the actual database to the website is simple. The connecting code will
be held within the HTML file. First thing to be created is the @if and else statement, the if
statement will have a <item> = null argument, followed by text that will signify that the database
is being loaded. Under the else statement the table will be created, due to the data being defined
specifically by name, each table that is created must have the names be a column. Create the
table row that will be the table header, which will contain the name of the data stored, such as
name, rainfall count, and landfall date. Once the name of each column is set, close the table
header. Open the table body and do @foreach (var name in _name) and within the brackets after
that, the table row which will display the desired data will be inputted. Each column will be
@name.(column name), and this will, when loaded onto the site, populate the table. Close the
table body and table and exit out of the else statement. Create an @code section, and instantiate a
variable with this line: private List<(database table name)> _name = new List<(database table
name)>();. This line will be called on in the earlier @foreach line. After this create a protected
override async task called OnInitializedAsync, and within this override _name will be used once
more. _name will be set to contain “await HttpClient.GetFromJsonAsync<List<(database table
name)>>(“api/(database)?numberOf(data)=(num)&startIndex=0”)”. This line is calling on Json
to pull information from the database, using the API to display how many rows of data should be
displayed (numberOf(data)=(num)) and where it should start(startIndex=0). Starting at 0 tells the
API to show the very first row, and the number inputted for num tells how many rows of data
will be displayed. See Appendix 4 for full code.


**Analysis**


The website itself, and execution of the code worked just as needed. The product design
had a starting page, introducing the purpose, where information was pulled from, and the names
of the hurricanes displayed. The second page would contain the base information, the Hurricane
Name (not sorted alphabetically), Hurricane Genesis Date, Hurricane Dissipation Date, and
Maximum Category reached. This is the base information displayed on the Historical Hurricane
Tracks database viewer, not including the track visualization. This specific website has no
additional features such as visualizations. See appendix 5 for full website visualization.
It was previously stated how the goal was for there to be more complex information, such
as movement speed, ocean temperatures, and size, displayed within the project. This was not
achieved, as at the current moment, with the time constrictions had, no more than the base
information plus maximum storm surge (which was one of the listed pieces of information) could
be inputted.
But the resulting website shows it is very much possible to create a website that unifies
all the information about a hurricane into one location. There can be different pages created that
have different dedications, and within the website there were three tables inputted into one page
that could be separated into two to three pages, and reorganized so that it is easier to read, use,
and understand. Each page could have an additional breakdown of the information and its
relevance, but that was not done. The website was able to load all information as long as
Postgres was opened, if put into public domain some work would need to be done to allow for
the data to be always displayed no matter the time of access.

_Limitations_

With the specific method that data was inputted into the database, the first limitation was
created. This limitation is that all data would be manually inputted, which is time consuming.
This method though would create the most accurate way to gather the data, as some of the data
doesn’t exist due to there being no measurements, manually finding, inputting, and saving all the
data can take multiple hours for a small set, such as the 20-set created. The best solution to this
issue would be to create a script to at least partially input data, such as Name, Category, Genesis
Date, Dissipation Date, and Max Wind Speed. This would cut back the time needed to input data
and could allow for a larger data set to work with.
Postgres, the app used to host the database, must always be open when executing the
code for the website. If it’s not open, opening the pages with the data will display an error code
of 500 with an Internal Server Error (ISE). On Mac, you must manually open Postgres, and if it
is not, the ISE will be thrown. This led to some delays as Postgres was not always remembered.
The display of my data is on an HTML/CSS website, which is good for the dataset size in
this specific case but could become quite messy with larger datasets. This would go against the
User-friendly goal, there are possible work arounds, including making more separated pages to
display the data, but all pages would be lengthy if the dataset was larger (assume 50+ rows).
Additionally, previously it was mentioned that the Hurricanes were not sorted based off
of an alphabetical system. This lack of organization causes more difficulty to find/index the
information, which goes back to the goals of the website to be easy to use for users. Without this
organization it possibly makes it more difficult/unappealing to use for users. It is a solvable
problem; it just takes some commands through Postgres or .NET to tell the code to execute in
alphabetical order.
Throughout development multiple issues would occur. The most major issue faced is that
my IDE, Rider by JetBrains, would not execute the code, saying .NET was not installed. Even
though it was installed on the device, Rider was not able to detect it, which would cause minor
delays in the coding. Development would continue in Rider, then it would sync to Visual Studio
to execute it. This would take more time than expected to be needed to develop the website.


**Conclusion**


Realistically, making an alternative display for Hurricane data is feasible, but to the
degree that NOAA does between their paper reports and multiple visualized databases would
take far longer than the time allotted for this project.
The overall goal of this project was to create something that can be a possible alternative
to NOAA’s multiple databases all with different sets of information. This alternative, although a
mere prototype, could be a way to allow for there to be a more centralized manner of displaying
all the data. With more work and time, this prototype could become a more organized way to
communicate hurricane information more effectively.
This website, with better formatting, more time, and additional features (such as
visualization, more interactivity), could potentially become an alternative to NOAA’s current
databases & displays for hurricane information. Not only could it display the information, but it
could also possibly rank some information such as size, windspeed, and storm surge, to display
which storm had the worst/largest of those categories.
